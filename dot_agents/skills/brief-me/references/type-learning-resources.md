# learning-resources — curricula, practice tools, and feedback methods for acquiring a practical skill, such as guitar accompaniment or drawing

## Default Diátaxis mode
`guide`: explain the selection criteria, compare resources, and show a complete practice example. Use `how-to` after the reader has chosen the resources; use `reference` for a resource directory.

## Worked-example shape
A **goal-to-practice walkthrough**: one concrete performance goal → prerequisite skills → named lessons and tools → a complete short practice session → observable progression criteria → a simple practice-log entry and the next session's decision. Show how to simplify the task when a prerequisite is missing. Label proposed schedules and sample logs as illustrative. Report only tool interactions actually exercised; the agent cannot claim to have acquired a human motor skill or observed the reader's progress.

## What counts as primary
1. Original studies for measured learning effects, with participants, task, and limits on transfer to the reader's situation.
2. Educators' own lessons and syllabi for what and how they teach. Teaching advice is attributed judgment unless independently evaluated.
3. Official product documentation, pricing, and access policies for capabilities and free-tier restrictions, checked on the research date. Store listings establish availability and listed terms, not educational effectiveness.
4. Qualified clinical or professional guidance for physical safety, with its scope stated.

Systematic reviews may support evidence synthesis but are labelled as secondary. Community reports may describe experience, not establish effectiveness. Affiliate rankings and vendor testimonials do not establish comparative quality.

## Natural islands
None by default. Use static comparison tables and a copyable practice log. Add an interactive element only when changing an input helps the reader make a genuine decision.

## Known traps
- Confusing a free trial or a few free lessons with an indefinitely usable curriculum; conflating free websites with paid companion apps.
- Treating musical knowledge as physical proficiency, or app recognition scores as proof of comfortable, efficient technique.
- Recommending many overlapping tools, promising a learning timeline without evidence, or presenting an illustrative routine as an experimentally validated prescription.
