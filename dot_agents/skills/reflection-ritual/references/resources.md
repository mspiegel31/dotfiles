# Resources: research & concepts behind the Reflection Ritual

Why each step exists, with links. Grouped by the idea it serves: the concept, the research (linked), and the ritual step that uses it.

Evidence grades: **meta** = meta-analysis; **study** = single empirical study; **classic** = foundational, replications mixed; **practice** = practitioner method, no causal test.

## 1. Keep the cadence

**Progress monitoring** (meta). [Harkin, Webb, …, Sheeran 2016, *Psychological Bulletin* 142(2)](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025) (paywalled). 138 RCTs, N=19,951: interventions that raise how often people check progress ↑ monitoring (d+≈1.98) and ↑ goal attainment (d+≈0.40); effects are larger when progress is *physically recorded*. → the fixed weekly slot, and the TickTick habit check-in as a self-recorded monitor.

**Habit formation** (study). [Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts, Wardle 2010, *EJSP* 40(6)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674) (paywalled). Automaticity asymptotes at a mean of ~66 days (range 18–254); *persistence* — not intensity — is what makes a habit stick. → same slot every week; "if a session balloons past 45 min it's a project — that's the failure mode."

**Specific goals + feedback** (meta). [Locke & Latham 2002, *American Psychologist* 57(9)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-0662.57.9.705) (paywalled). Specific, challenging goals with feedback beat "do your best" across decades of research. → each TickTick task is one clear, observable action, not an intention.

## 2. Turn insight into action (one next move)

**Implementation intentions** (meta). [Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1) (open access). 94 tests, d≈0.65: "if cue, then action" plans roughly double follow-through. → the if–then lives in the task's `--content`, not the title.

**Mental contrasting / WOOP** (meta). [Wang, Wang & Gai 2021, *Frontiers in Psychology* 12](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202) (open access). 24 studies, g≈0.34: WOOP (wish → outcome → obstacle → plan) helps, most when something interacts with you — the agent guiding this session. Underpinning: [Oettingen & Mayer 2002, *JPSP* 83(5)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.83.5.1198) (paywalled) — indulgent fantasy alone *reduces* effort; contrasting with the obstacle is what makes it work. → the Obstacle step is load-bearing, and a low score means drop/revise the goal, not force optimism.

## 3. Close open loops (open-loop sweep)

**Attention residue** (study). [Leroy 2009, *OBHDP* 109(2)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2009.04.002) (paywalled). Unfinished tasks leave residual attention that degrades the next task, and disengaging is hard. → the Do/Park/Kill verdict; closing the loop is the win.

**Zeigarnik effect** (classic). Zeigarnik 1927, *Psychologische Forschung* 9: unfinished tasks are remembered better than finished ones. Modern replications are mixed → treat as intuition-grade, not proof.

## 4. Strategy & career (monthly)

**Possible selves** (study). [Markus & Nurius 1986, *American Psychologist* 41(10)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-0662.41.10.436) (paywalled). People act toward hoped-for selves and away from feared ones. → the 3-sentence trajectory check ("if I keep this exact trajectory…").

**Future self-continuity** (study). [Hershfield 2011, *Annals of NYAS* 1235](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.2011.06201.x) (paywalled). The more continuous the future self feels, the better long-horizon decisions. → write the 12-month self in concrete terms, not abstractions.

## 5. Decide well (decision journal + 30/60/90)

**Hindsight bias** (classic). [Fischhoff 1975, *JEP:HPP* 1(3)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.1.3.288) (paywalled). Knowing the outcome silently inflates "I would have known" confidence. → the forecast (options, assumptions, confidence %) is written *before* the outcome.

**Planning fallacy / outside view** (study). [Kahneman & Lovallo 1993, *Management Science* 39(1)](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.39.1.17) (paywalled) and [Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, *JPSP* 67(3)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366) (paywalled). Inside-view estimates are overconfident; a reference-class check ("when did comparable efforts actually finish?") corrects them. → the 30/60/90 scan adjusts due dates rather than trusting the inside estimate.

**Premortem** (practice). [Klein 2007, *HBR*](https://hbr.org/2007/01/performing-a-project-premortem_9) — "assume it failed; what happened?" converts unexamined optimism into mitigations. Optional add-on for big monthly decisions.

## 6. The vault as a reflection medium

**Reflection on goals improves performance** (study). [Morisano, Hirsh, …, Shore 2010, *JAP* 95(2)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018478) (paywalled). Structured goal-setting + reflection beat a matched control over 4 months (direct but small educational sample). → combing the areas is reflection, not just filing.

**Cognitive offloading** (concept). Moving open loops into TickTick is "the brain is for having ideas, not holding them" made concrete. Nearest real evidence: attention residue (Leroy 2009) and the external-memory literature.

## Practice, not evidence (flagged honestly)

These shape the *workflow* but have no peer-reviewed causal test. The research above carries the load — don't cite these as proof.

- **GTD weekly review** — [gettingthingsdone.com](https://gettingthingsdone.com/). The cadence claim rests on Harkin 2016 (frequency + recording), not on GTD.
- **PARA** — [fortelabs.com](https://fortelabs.com/work/productivity/para-method/). Organizational structure; used as the vault's shape, not as an efficacy claim.

## Verifying the links

A DOI that returns HTTP 403 under a plain `curl` is a *paywalled* publisher page, not a dead link — the identifier is correct. Open-access ones (Gollwitzer 2006, Wang 2021) return 200. Re-verify any citation against its DOI before quoting an effect size.
